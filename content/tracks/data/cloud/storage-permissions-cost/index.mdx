<Section kind="intuition" title="Renting a warehouse by the shelf and by the visit">

In the cloud you do not buy disks. You rent <Term id="object-storage">object storage</Term> by the gigabyte per
month, and you pay query engines by the amount of data they read. That changes how pipelines are designed: a
layout decision that costs nothing on your laptop can cost thousands a month in the cloud.

It is like renting a storage facility that charges by the shelf each month and by the box every time someone
fetches one. Boxes you rarely open can move to a cheaper back room. The real money, though, often goes on staff
fetching every box for every question, when a well-labelled shelf would have let them grab just one.

The same facility needs keys. Each job and each person should get a key to the rooms they use and no others, so a
lost key or a clumsy worker can only do limited damage. That is <Term id="least-privilege">least privilege</Term>.

</Section>

<Section kind="technical" title="Storage classes, scan costs, permissions and configuration">

**Storage classes.** Providers sell several <Term id="storage-class">storage classes</Term>. Standard costs the most
per GB-month and is cheap to read. Infrequent access costs less to keep, charges per read and has a minimum
storage period. Archive is cheapest to keep, but objects must be restored (taking minutes to hours, depending on
the class) before they can be read. A lifecycle rule moves objects to a colder class automatically as they age.

**The bill after a year.** Suppose 500 GB lands every month and data older than 3 months moves to infrequent
access. In month 12 there are $3 \times 500 = 1{,}500$ GB hot and $9 \times 500 = 4{,}500$ GB cold. With the
lesson's example prices:

$$
\text{storage} = 1{,}500 \times 0.023 + 4{,}500 \times 0.0125 = 34.50 + 56.25 = 90.75 \text{ USD}
$$

$$
\text{queries} = q \times 30 \times \frac{\text{GB read per query}}{1000} \times 5 \text{ USD per TB}
$$

A dashboard running 100 queries a day that each read the last 7 days ($500 \times 7 / 30 \approx 117$ GB) costs
$100 \times 30 \times 0.117 \times 5 = 1{,}750$ USD. If each query reads the whole 6,000 GB table instead, it costs
90,000 USD. Storage is rarely the expensive part; reading is. Partitioning and column pruning from the
[lakehouse lesson](#/learn/data/storage/warehouse-lake-lakehouse) are cost controls.

The third cost is <Term id="egress">egress</Term>: data moved out of a region or provider is billed per GB, while
moving data in is usually free. Run compute in the same region as the data.

**Permissions.** Every request is made by an identity (a person, or a role or service account for a job), for an
action, on a resource. Cloud IAM evaluates the applicable policies in a fixed order:

1. If any statement explicitly **denies** the request, it is denied.
2. Otherwise, if any statement **allows** it, it is allowed.
3. Otherwise it is denied by default.

Least privilege means one identity per job, actions listed explicitly, resources scoped to prefixes, and an
explicit deny protecting what must never change, such as the raw data you replay from.

**Configuration and secrets.** The same pipeline code is deployed to development, staging and production; only its
configuration differs (bucket names, database hosts). Read configuration from the environment, and get credentials
from the identity the job runs as, or from a secrets manager. Never put keys or passwords in code.

<Callout kind="simplification">
  The prices are example values chosen as round numbers, not any provider's current price list, and the model leaves
  out per-request charges, retrieval fees, minimum storage durations, free tiers and discounts. Real IAM also has
  resource policies, permission boundaries and conditions. Use the formula with your provider's current prices.
</Callout>

</Section>

<Section kind="mistakes" title="Bills and breaches that start small">

<CommonMistake
  title="SELECT * on a big table behind a dashboard"
  wrong={"SELECT * FROM events WHERE country = 'NO'  -- refreshed every 5 minutes"}
  error="(no error: each refresh scans every column of every day, and the bill arrives at the end of the month)"
  cause="Serverless engines bill by bytes read. SELECT * reads all columns, and a filter on a non-partition column reads all files."
  fix={"SELECT day, SUM(amount) FROM events\nWHERE day >= CURRENT_DATE - INTERVAL 7 DAY AND country = 'NO'\nGROUP BY day"}
/>

<CommonMistake
  title="Access keys in the repository"
  wrong={"s3 = boto3.client(\"s3\", aws_access_key_id=\"AKIA...\", aws_secret_access_key=\"...\")"}
  error="(no error until someone finds the key in the git history, which automated scanners do within minutes of a public push)"
  cause="Anything committed stays in every clone and in history, even after the line is deleted."
  fix={"s3 = boto3.client(\"s3\")  # credentials from the job's role; rotate any key that was ever committed"}
/>

<CommonMistake
  title="A public bucket to make sharing easy"
  wrong={"bucket policy: Allow, Principal \"*\", Action s3:GetObject, Resource acme-lake/*"}
  error="(no error: anyone on the internet can download every object, including customer data)"
  cause={"\"*\" as a principal means everyone, not everyone in the company."}
  fix={"grant a specific role or group read access to one prefix, and keep the provider's block-public-access setting on"}
/>

<Section kind="practice" title="Running data in the cloud">

- **Tag and budget.** Tag buckets, warehouses and jobs by team and pipeline, set budget alerts, and look at the
  biggest line items monthly. Query scans and forgotten clusters usually top the list.
- **Lifecycle rules from day one.** Raw landing data older than a few months can move to a colder class or be
  deleted when nothing replays it; check minimum storage periods and retrieval fees first.
- **One role per job, reviewed regularly.** Compare granted permissions with what audit logs show was used, and
  remove the rest.
- **Deploy like software.** The pipeline's code, infrastructure (buckets, roles, schedules) and configuration live
  in version control and are deployed per environment by CI, with secrets injected at run time.
- **For ML,** training data is often the most sensitive data a company has. Give training jobs read access to
  exactly the feature tables they use, and keep personal data out of them where you can.

</Section>

<Section kind="summary">

- Object storage is billed per GB-month by storage class; lifecycle rules move old data to cheaper classes, and
  archived data must be restored before it can be read.
- Query engines bill by bytes read, so partitioning and selecting columns are usually the biggest cost levers.
- Permissions: explicit deny wins, then allow, otherwise denied. Give each job its own narrowly scoped identity.
- Keep configuration in the environment and credentials in roles or a secrets manager, never in code.
