<Section kind="intuition" title="Look before you model">

Before you train anything on the penguins, you want to know what is in the data: how many penguins of each
species, how big they are, which measurements move together, and whether anything looks odd. That habit is
<Term id="exploratory-data-analysis">exploratory data analysis</Term> (EDA): summaries, counts, comparisons
between groups and plots, with a notebook open to write down what you notice.

EDA has one trap that this lesson is built around. Plot bill length against bill depth for all penguins and the
cloud slopes downward: longer bills seem to be shallower. Split the same points by species and every species
slopes upward. Both pictures are correct. They answer different questions, and only one of them describes a
penguin of a given species. That reversal is called <Term id="simpsons-paradox">Simpson’s paradox</Term>.

</Section>

<Section kind="technical" title="Four summaries and one number for relationships">

Each summary answers a question. Here they are on the full dataset (344 rows), run locally with pandas 3.0.2:

- **`df.describe()`**: per number column, the count of values present, the <Term id="mean">mean</Term>, the
  <Term id="standard-deviation">standard deviation</Term> (pandas divides by $n - 1$), the minimum, the quartiles
  and the maximum. Bill length has count 342: the two empty rows from the previous lesson are skipped.
- **`df["species"].value_counts()`**: rows per category: Adelie 152, Gentoo 124, Chinstrap 68. Unequal groups
  matter later: a model that always answers "Adelie" is already right $152 / 344 \approx 44\%$ of the time.
- **`df.groupby("species")[cols].mean()`**: the mean of each column per species. Mean bill length is 38.8 mm for
  Adelie, 48.8 for Chinstrap and 47.5 for Gentoo; mean bill depth is 18.3, 18.4 and 15.0 mm. Gentoo bills are long
  and shallow.
- **Correlation**: how two columns move together.

**Pearson’s r.** For pairs $(x_i, y_i)$ with means $\bar{x}$ and $\bar{y}$, the
<Term id="correlation">correlation</Term> is

$$
r = \frac{\sum_i (x_i - \bar{x})(y_i - \bar{y})}{\sqrt{\sum_i (x_i - \bar{x})^2 \cdot \sum_i (y_i - \bar{y})^2}}
$$

Each penguin adds a positive product when it is above both means or below both, and a negative one when it is
above one mean and below the other. The denominator removes the units, so $-1 \le r \le 1$: $1$ is a perfect
rising line, $-1$ a perfect falling line, and $0$ no straight-line trend at all.

**Worked example.** Three points $(1, 2), (2, 4), (3, 5)$: $\bar{x} = 2$, $\bar{y} = 11/3$. The deviations
of $y$ are $-5/3, 1/3, 4/3$, so

$$
r = \frac{(-1)(-\tfrac{5}{3}) + 0 + (1)(\tfrac{4}{3})}{\sqrt{2 \cdot \tfrac{42}{9}}} = \frac{3}{\sqrt{9.333}} \approx 0.982
$$

**Simpson’s paradox in numbers.** For bill length against bill depth on the 342 complete rows, pandas gives
$r = -0.235$ overall, but $0.391$ for Adelie, $0.654$ for Chinstrap and $0.643$ for Gentoo. Species is a
<Term id="confounder">confounder</Term> here: it affects both columns. Adelie sit at short and deep, Gentoo at
long and shallow, so the pooled cloud runs from top left to bottom right even though each species runs upward.

<Callout kind="simplification">
  Pearson’s r only sees straight-line trends and is pulled around by outliers. For curved or rank-based
  relationships, pandas also offers `method="spearman"`. And no correlation, pooled or per group, says that one
  measurement causes the other.
</Callout>

</Section>

<Section kind="mistakes" title="Text columns in numeric summaries">

<CommonMistake
  title="Correlating a whole DataFrame that has text columns"
  wrong={'df.corr()'}
  error="ValueError: could not convert string to float: 'Adelie'"
  cause="`corr` needs numbers, and `species`, `island` and `sex` are text. pandas 3 does not drop them silently."
  fix={'num_cols = ["bill_length_mm", "bill_depth_mm", "flipper_length_mm", "body_mass_g"]\ndf[num_cols].corr()'}
/>

<CommonMistake
  title="Averaging every column per group"
  wrong={'df.groupby("species").mean()'}
  error="TypeError: dtype 'str' does not support operation 'mean'"
  cause="After grouping by species, `island` and `sex` are still text columns, and a text column has no mean."
  fix={'df.groupby("species")[num_cols].mean()\n# or: df.groupby("species").mean(numeric_only=True)'}
/>

<CommonMistake
  title="Passing a column name to Series.corr"
  wrong={'df["bill_length_mm"].corr("bill_depth_mm")'}
  error="TypeError: unsupported type: <class 'str'>"
  cause="`Series.corr` takes another Series, not the name of a column."
  fix={'df["bill_length_mm"].corr(df["bill_depth_mm"])'}
/>

<Callout kind="warning">
  The worst EDA mistake raises no error: reporting a pooled correlation for data made of different groups.
  Check every relationship within the obvious groups (species, island, sex) before you write it down.
</Callout>

</Section>

<Section kind="practice" title="Write down questions and hypotheses">

EDA ends with a written list, not a model. Each entry says what you saw, what you suspect, and how you would
check it on data you haven't browsed. For the penguins:

| Observation                                              | Question or hypothesis                                      | How to check                                                          |
| -------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Bill depth vs length: $r = -0.235$ overall, positive within species | Species, not bill shape, drives the pooled trend   | Compare r within species; color plots by species                      |
| Gentoo have long, shallow bills                          | Bill depth and length together could separate the species  | Train a classifier on those two features and measure accuracy         |
| Adelie 152, Gentoo 124, Chinstrap 68                     | Chinstrap may be hardest to learn                           | Look at per-species errors, not just overall accuracy                 |
| Flipper length and body mass: $r = 0.871$                | One of them may add little once the other is known         | Compare models with one, the other, and both                          |
| Gentoo live only on Biscoe, Chinstrap only on Dream      | Island alone may "predict" species in this sample          | Ask whether a new sample would keep that pattern before using island |

Two cautions. A pattern found by browsing many plots can be luck, so confirm it on data you set aside before
exploring ([Train, validation and test sets](#/learn/ml/classical/splits-leakage)). And a hypothesis that can't
be checked ("penguins prefer deep bills") belongs in a different list.

</Section>

<Section kind="summary">

- Start with `describe()`, `value_counts()` and `groupby(...).mean()`; check the counts behind every number.
- Pearson’s r measures straight-line association, from −1 to 1, and has no units.
- A pooled correlation can reverse inside every group (Simpson’s paradox): bill length vs depth is −0.235
  overall and positive for each species.
- Compute statistics on number columns only; pandas 3 raises on text columns.
- End EDA with written, testable questions. Correlation is not causation.
