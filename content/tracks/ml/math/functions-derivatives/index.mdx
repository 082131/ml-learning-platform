<Section kind="intuition" title="How steep is the road right here?">

Driving up a hill, the road is steeper in some places than others. "How steep is it right here?" has an answer at
every point, even though the road curves. You could estimate it by measuring how much you climb over the next
10 meters, then over the next 1 meter, then over the next 10 centimeters. The answers settle down to one number:
the slope at that point.

That number is the <Term id="derivative">derivative</Term>. Training a model is mostly about derivatives: the
model's error is a <Term id="function">function</Term> of its weights, and the derivative says which way to
change a weight so the error goes down, and how strongly the error responds.

</Section>

<Section kind="technical" title="From secant to tangent">

**Functions.** A function $f$ is a rule that turns an input $x$ into an output $f(x)$. For $f(x) = x^2$,
$f(3) = 9$. Plotting every $(x, f(x))$ gives its graph.

**Slope of a secant.** Between $x$ and $x + h$ the graph rises by $f(x + h) - f(x)$ over a run of $h$:

$$
\text{slope of the secant} = \frac{f(x + h) - f(x)}{h}
$$

For $f(x) = x^2$ at $x = 1$ with $h = 0.1$: $\dfrac{1.1^2 - 1^2}{0.1} = \dfrac{1.21 - 1}{0.1} = 2.1$.

**The derivative** is the value this slope approaches as $h$ shrinks toward 0:

$$
f'(x) = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(x + h) - f(x)}{h}
$$

For $x^2$: $\dfrac{(x + h)^2 - x^2}{h} = \dfrac{2xh + h^2}{h} = 2x + h$, which approaches $2x$. So $f'(1) = 2$,
and the secant with $h = 0.1$ was off by exactly $h$. The line through $(x, f(x))$ with slope $f'(x)$ is the
<Term id="tangent-line">tangent line</Term>: the straight line that best matches the graph near $x$.

**Rules** that cover most of what ML needs at first:

| Rule              | Function           | Derivative               |
| ----------------- | ------------------ | ------------------------ |
| Constant          | $c$                | $0$                      |
| Power             | $x^n$              | $n x^{n-1}$              |
| Constant multiple | $c \, g(x)$        | $c \, g'(x)$             |
| Sum               | $g(x) + k(x)$      | $g'(x) + k'(x)$          |

So $f(x) = x^3 - 3x$ has $f'(x) = 3x^2 - 3$: at $x = 2$ the slope is $9$, and at $x = 1$ it is $0$, a flat spot
(here a local minimum).

**Reading the sign.** $f'(x) > 0$: $f$ goes up as $x$ grows. $f'(x) < 0$: it goes down. $f'(x) = 0$: flat, as at
a minimum or maximum. Near $x$, $f(x + h) \approx f(x) + f'(x)\,h$.

**Numerical derivatives** use a small but nonzero $h$ (a <Term id="finite-difference">finite difference</Term>).
The central difference $\dfrac{f(x + h) - f(x - h)}{2h}$ is usually far more accurate than the forward one: for
$x^2$ it gives exactly $2x$ for any $h$.

<Callout kind="simplification">
  Not every function has a derivative everywhere. $|x|$ has a corner at 0, where the slope jumps from −1 to 1.
  ML uses such functions anyway (ReLU, $\max(0, x)$, has the same kind of corner), and libraries simply pick a
  value there by convention.
</Callout>

</Section>

<Section kind="mistakes" title="Steps that are too small, or zero">

<CommonMistake
  title="Using a step of exactly 0"
  wrong={'def f(x):\n    return x * x\n\nx, h = 1.0, 0.0\n(f(x + h) - f(x)) / h'}
  error="ZeroDivisionError: float division by zero"
  cause="The derivative is a limit as h approaches 0, not the value at h = 0. With h = 0 the secant has no run, so the slope is 0 / 0."
  fix={'h = 1e-5\n(f(x + h) - f(x - h)) / (2 * h)  # central difference with a small, nonzero h'}
/>

<Callout kind="warning">
  Smaller is not always better. Floats keep about 16 significant digits, so $f(x + h) - f(x)$ with $h = 10^{-12}$
  keeps only about 4 correct digits, and dividing by $h$ magnifies the rest: the sweep above shows an error of
  $1.8 \times 10^{-4}$ there, worse than with $h = 10^{-6}$. For central differences, $h$ around $10^{-5}$ is a
  good default.
</Callout>

<Callout kind="warning">
  Watch the parentheses. `f(x + h) - f(x) / h` divides only $f(x)$ by $h$: for $x^2$ at $x = 1$, $h = 0.1$ it
  gives $1.21 - 10 = -8.79$ instead of $2.1$, with no error message.
</Callout>

</Section>

<Section kind="summary">

- A function maps an input to an output; its derivative $f'(x)$ is the slope of the tangent at $x$.
- $f'(x)$ is the limit of the secant slope $(f(x + h) - f(x)) / h$ as $h \to 0$.
- Power rule: $x^n \to n x^{n-1}$; derivatives of sums and constant multiples work term by term.
- The sign of $f'$ tells you whether $f$ rises or falls; $f'(x) = 0$ at flat points.
- Numerical derivatives need a small, nonzero $h$; central differences are more accurate, and $h$ near
  $10^{-5}$ avoids round-off.

many inputs.

</Section>
