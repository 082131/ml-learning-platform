<Section kind="intuition" title="An open-book exam for a language model">

A language model answers from what it absorbed during training. It has never read your company's help center,
yesterday's policy change or a customer's contract, and when it doesn't know, it may still produce a fluent,
confident answer.

<Term id="retrieval-augmented-generation">Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG)</Term> turns the closed-book exam
into an open-book one. Before the model answers, your code looks up the few passages most likely to contain the
answer and puts them into the prompt, with an instruction: answer only from these, cite them, and say so if the
answer isn't there.

The quality of the answer now depends on two things you control: whether the right passage was found, and whether
the model used it faithfully. Most RAG failures in practice are retrieval failures, so this lesson spends most of its
time there.

The documents in this lesson are the help center of Lumen Notes, a note-taking app invented for this course: seven
short articles on plans, refunds, payments, security, email, sync errors and the API.

</Section>

<Section kind="technical" title="Chunk, retrieve, rerank, assemble, generate">

**Indexing (once, ahead of time).**

1. **Chunk.** <Term id="chunking">Chunking</Term> splits each document into windows of `size` words; consecutive
   windows share `overlap` words, so a window starts every `size − overlap` words. A document of 100 words with size
   40 and overlap 10 gives chunks starting at words 0, 30 and 60.
2. **Embed.** Each chunk gets a 384-number embedding from all-MiniLM-L6-v2, as in
   [the previous lesson](#/learn/ai/representations/tokens-embeddings). The vectors for every chunk setting in this
   lesson were computed ahead of time; the chunking itself runs live, and each chunk's text is looked up in the
   bundled table.

**Answering (for every question).**

3. **Retrieve.** Embed the question and compute its <Term id="cosine-similarity">cosine similarity</Term> with
   every chunk. Keep the best 8 as candidates.
4. **Rerank.** <Term id="reranking">Reranking</Term> re-scores only those candidates with a second method. Here
   the second method is <Term id="bm25">BM25</Term>, a keyword score that rewards rare words of the question found in
   the chunk. The two are mixed with a weight $w$:

   $$
   \text{score} = (1 - w)\cdot\cos(q, c) + w \cdot \frac{\text{BM25}(q, c)}{\max_{c'} \text{BM25}(q, c')}
   $$

   Dividing by the largest BM25 among the candidates puts the keyword score on a 0 to 1 scale, like the cosine.
   With $w = 0$ the cosine order is kept. The top $k$ chunks after reranking go on.
5. **Assemble.** Build one prompt: instructions, the $k$ chunks numbered [1] to [k] with their article titles,
   and the question. This makes the answer <Term id="grounding">grounded</Term> and checkable.
6. **Generate.** The language model writes the answer. A good answer cites [n] for each claim; your code can check
   that every cited number exists and that the cited chunk supports the claim.

<Callout kind="simplification">
  Three shortcuts keep this lesson runnable in a browser. Chunks are counted in words; production systems count
  tokens, and often split on paragraphs or headings first. The reranker is a keyword mix; production rerankers are
  usually cross-encoder models that read the question and the chunk together, which is more accurate and slower
  (see the Sentence Transformers reference). And no language model runs here: the response is scripted, as its label
  says. Retrieval, reranking and the prompt are computed for real.
</Callout>

</Section>

Things to try, starting from the question "What does error E-402 mean?" with 40-word chunks and 10 words of overlap:

- Set the keyword weight to 0 and k to 1. Cosine similarity alone ranks a chunk about **payment** error B-402 first,
  and the E-402 chunk second, so the prompt holds the wrong passage and the response says the context doesn't
  explain E-402. A real model given that prompt could also wrongly describe B-402.
- Raise the keyword weight back to 0.5. The term "e-402" appears in only one chunk, BM25 rewards it, and the right
  chunk moves to rank 1.
- Switch to 80-word chunks with the keyword weight at 0. The answer chunk falls to cosine rank 3: that chunk also covers
  offline editing, conflicted copies and error E-409, which dilutes its embedding.
- Ask "Can Lumen turn my handwriting into text?". No chunk answers it, and the grounded response says so instead of
  inventing a feature. Raising k doesn't help: retrieval can only find what the documents contain.
- Try "What happens if I edit the same note on my laptop and phone while both are offline?" with 20-word chunks and
  10 words of overlap, keyword weight 0 and k = 3: the answer chunk ranks 4th and is left out.

<Section kind="mistakes" title="Splitting facts, and trusting the answer">

<CommonMistake
  title="Chunks that cut a fact in half"
  wrong={'chunks = chunk_words(text, size=8, overlap=0)\n# chunk 2: "time from Settings, Billing. Monthly plans are not"\n# chunk 3: "refunded."'}
  error="(no error: questions about refunds retrieve a chunk that doesn't say plans aren't refunded)"
  cause="A fixed-size window ignores sentence boundaries. The fact sits across two chunks, so no single chunk contains it and neither embedding represents it well."
  fix={'chunks = chunk_words(text, size=8, overlap=2)\n# "Monthly plans are not refunded." now appears whole in one chunk'}
/>

<CommonMistake
  title="Showing the answer without checking its sources"
  wrong={'answer = llm(prompt)\nreturn answer  # shown to the user as is'}
  error="(no error: an answer that cites [4] when only 3 sources were sent, or cites a source that says something else, reaches the user)"
  cause="Grounding instructions make made-up answers less likely, not impossible. A model can cite a source that doesn't support the claim, or answer from its training data."
  fix={'cited = {int(n) for n in re.findall(r"\\[(\\d+)\\]", answer)}\nif not cited or not cited <= set(range(1, k + 1)):\n    return "I couldn\'t find a sourced answer to that."'}
/>

<Callout kind="warning">
  Documents you retrieve are text the model will read as part of its instructions. A document that says "ignore the
  previous instructions" is a prompt-injection risk. Treat retrieved text as data, and limit what an answer can
  trigger.
</Callout>

</Section>

<Section kind="practice" title="Tuning a RAG system for real">

When a RAG answer is wrong, find which stage failed before changing anything:

| Symptom                                                   | Likely stage   | What to try                                                 |
| --------------------------------------------------------- | -------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| The answer passage exists but isn't in the prompt         | Retrieval      | Hybrid keyword search, a reranker, a larger k              |
| The passage is in the prompt but split or diluted         | Chunking       | Smaller chunks, overlap, splitting on headings              |
| The passage is in the prompt and the answer is still wrong | Generation     | Clearer instructions, fewer distracting chunks, check citations |
| No document contains the answer                            | Content        | Add the content; make the model say it doesn't know         |

Measure before tuning: write 20 to 50 real questions with the passage that answers each, and count how often that
passage reaches the prompt (recall at k). [Vector databases, chunking, reranking and retrieval
evaluation](#/learn/ai/rag/vector-db-eval) builds that evaluation.

<Section kind="summary">

- RAG retrieves passages from your documents and puts them in the prompt; the model is not retrained.
- Chunk size trades precision for context; overlap keeps facts near a boundary in one piece.
- Retrieval by cosine similarity finds paraphrases but blurs exact codes; a keyword score (BM25) catches them.
  Reranking mixes a second score over the first stage's few candidates.
- The prompt numbers its sources so answers can be cited and checked, and tells the model to say when the answer
  isn't there.
- Diagnose failures stage by stage: content, chunking, retrieval, then generation.
