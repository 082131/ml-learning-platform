<Section kind="intuition" title="Look before you summarize">

<Term id="exploratory-data-analysis">Exploratory data analysis</Term> (EDA) is the step between a clean table
and a conclusion. Its job is to find out what the data actually looks like, so that the numbers you later report
mean what you think they mean.

A single number such as "the average session is 52.7 minutes" can describe a typical user, or be dragged up by
a few people who left a tab open all day, or sit in the gap between two very different groups. Only the shape of
the data tells you which. So EDA always pairs a statistic with a picture, and every chart starts from a
question.

</Section>

<Section kind="technical" title="Statistics that fit the shape, charts that fit the question">

**Center and spread.** The mean is the balance point; the <Term id="median">median</Term> is the middle value.
For symmetric data they agree. When a long tail pulls them apart
(<Term id="skewness">skewness</Term>), report the median, and the IQR (width of the middle 50%) instead of the
standard deviation, which squares distances and is dominated by extremes. pandas’ `describe()` gives count, mean,
std, min, quartiles and max in one call; `std()` in pandas uses $n - 1$ (`ddof=1`), NumPy’s uses $n$ unless you
pass `ddof=1`.

**One chart per question.**

| Question | Chart | Matplotlib / pandas |
|---|---|---|
| How is one variable distributed? | Histogram, box plot | `ax.hist(x, bins=30)`, `df.boxplot(column)` |
| How do groups compare? | Box plots or histograms per group on one axis; bars for one number per group | `df.boxplot(column, by="group")`, `ax.bar` |
| How do two numeric variables relate? | Scatter plot | `ax.scatter(x, y, alpha=0.5)` |
| How does something change over time? | Line chart | `ax.plot(dates, values)` |

Label axes with units, start bar charts at zero, and use a log scale for strongly right-skewed data. Bins change
how a histogram looks: try a few.

**Relationships.** `df.corr()` gives Pearson <Term id="correlation">correlation</Term> $r$ between numeric
columns: $+1$ and $-1$ are perfect straight lines, 0 is no **linear** trend. Correlation misses curves, can be
created or destroyed by one extreme point, and never says which variable causes the other. Spearman correlation
(the correlation of ranks) is less swayed by extremes. Look at the scatter plot every time.

**Write it down.** Each EDA step is a question, the chart or statistic that answers it, and one sentence of
finding with numbers, such as "pro users spend a median of 51.9 minutes a month, about twice free users’ 24.8".

</Section>

<Section kind="mistakes" title="Misleading summaries and charts">

<CommonMistake
  title="Reporting the mean of skewed data"
  wrong={'print("typical income:", df["income"].mean())'}
  error="No error: the number is real but describes almost nobody."
  cause="A few large values pull the mean far above the typical value."
  fix={'print("median income:", df["income"].median(), "| mean:", df["income"].mean())'}
/>

<CommonMistake
  title="A bar chart that does not start at zero"
  wrong={'ax.bar(groups, means)\nax.set_ylim(48, 52)'}
  error="No error: a 2% difference looks like a fourfold one."
  cause="Bar length encodes the value, so a cut axis exaggerates differences."
  fix={'ax.bar(groups, means)   # keep the zero baseline\n# or show the points with error bars instead'}
/>

<CommonMistake
  title="Trusting r without a plot"
  wrong={'if abs(df["x"].corr(df["y"])) < 0.1:\n    print("x does not matter")'}
  error="No error: a strong curved relationship has r close to 0."
  cause="Pearson r only measures straight-line association."
  fix={'df.plot.scatter(x="x", y="y")   # look first, then decide'}
/>

</Section>

<Section kind="practice" title="Where you will meet it">

| In data and ML code | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| `df.describe()` and `df.isna().mean()` | First overview of every column |
| `df["target"].value_counts(normalize=True)` | Class balance before training |
| `df.hist(bins=30, figsize=(12, 8))` | A histogram for every numeric column at once |
| `df.corr(numeric_only=True)` | Candidate features and redundant pairs |
| Box plot of the target per category | Which categories differ |

<Section kind="summary">

- Match the statistic to the shape: median and IQR for skewed data or data with extremes.
- Choose the chart from the question: distribution, comparison, relationship or trend.
- Correlation measures only linear association, is swayed by single points, and never shows causation.
- Record each finding as a question, a chart or number, and one sentence.
