<Section kind="intuition" title="Models go stale; the question is when to notice">

A model is trained on a snapshot of the past and deployed into a future that keeps moving. Customers change, products
change, fraudsters change. Retraining fixes this, but each run costs compute, review time and the risk of shipping a
worse model. Retrain too rarely and the model drifts out of date; retrain constantly and you pay for runs that change
nothing.

Teams answer this with a continuous-training pipeline that runs the same steps every time, and a trigger that decides
when to run it. This lesson compares the common triggers on one year of simulated drift, with a slow trend and one
sudden shift, and shows why the best trigger depends on how quickly you can see the truth.

</Section>

<Section kind="technical" title="The pipeline, four triggers and label delay">

**The pipeline.** <Term id="continuous-training">Continuous training</Term> automates what a person would otherwise do
by hand:

1. **Trigger** (below) starts a run.
2. **Snapshot**: pin the data version ([snapshots and time travel](#/learn/dataops/versioning/data-snapshots)), so every
   later step sees the same rows.
3. **Validate**: schema and drift checks against the baseline
   ([baselines and drift](#/learn/dataops/validation/baselines-drift)); broken data stops the run.
4. **Train** with versioned code and config.
5. **Evaluate**: compare the candidate with the production model on the newest labeled data and on important slices.
6. **Gate**: promote only if the candidate beats production by a margin and no slice regresses
   (a <Term id="release-gate">release gate</Term>); register it with its data version, metrics and trigger.

**Four triggers.** A <Term id="retraining-trigger">retraining trigger</Term> is one of:

| Trigger | Watches | Strength | Weakness |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Schedule | the calendar | simple, predictable cost | blind to sudden changes; may run for nothing |
| Data volume | new labeled rows | uses data as it arrives | blind to whether anything changed |
| Input drift | $P(x)$ against the baseline | reacts the same day | misses concept drift; can fire on harmless shifts |
| Performance | accuracy on new labels | watches what matters | late by the label delay |

**Label delay.** The <Term id="label-delay">label delay</Term> is the time from a prediction to knowing its true
outcome: seconds for a click, weeks for a fraud chargeback, months for a loan default. A performance trigger with
delay $L$ sees week $w$'s accuracy only in week $w + L$.

**The decay model used below.** The data moves 1 unit per week, and jumps 30 units in week 33. A model trained in week
$t$ has accuracy

$$
\text{acc}(w) = 0.92 - 0.004 \cdot \lvert \text{world}(w) - \text{world}(t) \rvert
$$

This is a model chosen to be simple and stated in full, not a measurement: real models decay unevenly, and some barely
decay at all.

</Section>

Things to try:

- **Never retrain:** accuracy slides to 0.596 by the end of the year; mean 0.7742.
- **Quarterly** (default): 3 runs, mean 0.8822. The shift in week 33 waits until week 39: worst week 0.752.
- **Monthly:** 12 runs, mean 0.9071, but weeks 33 to 35 still run a pre-shift model (0.788).
- **Input drift:** 5 runs, mean 0.9032, nearly as good as monthly with fewer than half the runs. It retrains in
  week 33 itself, so the worst week is 0.884.
- **Accuracy trigger, then raise the label delay:** with no delay it retrains in week 33 (mean 0.9009). With a 2-week
  delay it reacts in week 35 and the worst week falls to 0.768; with 6 weeks, to 0.736.

<Section kind="mistakes" title="Triggers that fire wrong">

<CommonMistake
  title="Retraining on every drift alert without validation"
  wrong={"if psi > 0.25:\n    train_and_deploy(latest_data)"}
  error="(no error: a unit bug upstream triggers a retrain, and the new model learns the bug)"
  cause="Drift alerts fire on broken pipelines as well as real change."
  fix="validate the snapshot first, and deploy only through the evaluation gate"
/>

<CommonMistake
  title="Evaluating the candidate on data the production model never had a chance on"
  wrong={"new = accuracy(candidate, candidate_train_holdout)\nold = accuracy(production, production_train_holdout)"}
  error="(no error: the comparison is meaningless because the two numbers come from different data)"
  cause="Each model is scored on its own held-out set, from different time periods."
  fix="score both models on the same, newest labeled data (and on fixed gold slices)"
/>

<CommonMistake
  title="Relying only on a performance trigger with long label delay"
  wrong={"retrain_when(accuracy_last_week() < 0.88)   # chargebacks arrive after 60 days"}
  error="(no error: two months of bad fraud decisions before the trigger can see anything)"
  cause="Performance can only be measured once labels arrive."
  fix="add input-drift and prediction-distribution monitors as early warnings, and keep a schedule as a backstop"
/>

<Section kind="practice" title="Running continuous training">

- **Start with a schedule plus alerts.** A simple schedule, with drift and performance monitors that alert a person.
  Automate triggers once you trust the signals.
- **Make the pipeline the only way to ship.** Manual retrains skip validation and lose lineage.
- **Gate on fixed criteria** written down before the run: margin, slices, latency, size.
- **Record why each run happened:** the trigger, data version, metrics and decision go into the registry.
- **Measure the label delay** for your problem; it decides how much you can rely on performance triggers.
- **Track the cost:** runs per month, compute per run, and how often the gate rejects. Frequent rejections mean the
  trigger fires for nothing.

</Section>

<Section kind="summary">

- Continuous training runs the same steps every time: snapshot, validate, train, evaluate, gate, register.
- Schedules are blind to sudden changes; input-drift triggers react at once but miss concept drift; performance
  triggers watch what matters but lag by the label delay.
- In the simulation, a drift trigger matched monthly retraining's mean accuracy with fewer than half the runs, and
  avoided its worst week.
- A gate that compares candidate and production on the same newest data keeps automation from shipping regressions.
