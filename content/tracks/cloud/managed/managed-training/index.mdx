<Section kind="intuition" title="Renting a kitchen versus ordering from a caterer">

In the last three lessons you trained the hard way: pick a GPU instance, start it, install drivers, copy the data,
run the script, write checkpoints, copy the model out, and remember to switch the machine off. That is like renting a
kitchen. You get full control, and you also do the shopping, the cooking and the washing up, and you pay for the
kitchen until you hand back the key.

A <Term id="managed-training-job">managed training job</Term> is the caterer. You hand over a recipe (your training
script), say how big the order is (the instance type and count) and where the ingredients are (a bucket path). The
service finds the machines, brings the data, runs the script, collects the result into your bucket, and leaves. You
pay for the cooking, not for the hours the kitchen sat empty.

This lesson answers three questions in order: what exactly the service does for you (and what you give up), where
the money goes in a job's life, and what your script must do so that the same code runs on a laptop, on SageMaker
and on Vertex AI.

</Section>

<Section kind="technical" title="What a training job does, phase by phase">

**The big picture.** You describe the job (script, container, instances, data, output) → the service provisions
machines, unbilled → it copies or mounts your data, billed → your script runs against fixed paths → it uploads
whatever the script left in the model folder, billed → and it tears everything down. Every saving and every surprise
in this lesson comes from one of those steps.

### What "managed" buys you, and what it costs

On AWS the service is **Amazon SageMaker AI** training jobs; on Google Cloud it is **Vertex AI custom training**. In
both, a job is a request, not a machine: you submit it, it runs, it ends. That changes four things compared with the
VM you ran yourself.

1. **No idle machines.** The instances exist only for the job. There is no "forgot to stop it" bill, which in
   practice is the biggest single saving, larger than any discount.
2. **A known environment.** You pick a prebuilt framework container (PyTorch 2.5 with CUDA, for example) or bring
   your own image. No driver installation, and the same image every time, which makes runs repeatable.
3. **Data in, model out, logs and metrics, by contract.** The service copies data to known paths, uploads the model
   folder, streams logs to CloudWatch or Cloud Logging, and records every job's settings and timings.
4. **Permissions through a role.** The job runs as an <Term id="iam-role">IAM role</Term> (SageMaker) or a service
   account (Vertex AI), exactly as in the IAM lesson. Nothing on the machine holds long-lived keys.

What you give up is the interactive machine. You cannot SSH in halfway through and poke around (SageMaker offers
remote debugging and Vertex AI an interactive shell, but they are deliberate extras), each job pays a few minutes of
start-up before your first line runs, and the instance price for the `ml.` instance types is somewhat higher than the
same EC2 type. So managed jobs win when runs are repeatable and the machines would otherwise sit idle; a VM wins for
long interactive exploration on one box.

### The life of a SageMaker job, and which minutes are billed

A training job moves through visible states, which `DescribeTrainingJob` reports with timestamps:

| Phase | What happens | Billed? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Starting** | Find capacity, launch instances, pull the training image | No |
| **Downloading** | Copy the input data to the instances (File mode) | Yes |
| **Training** | Run your script | Yes |
| **Uploading** | Pack `/opt/ml/model` into `model.tar.gz` and copy it to S3 | Yes |

The billed window runs from `TrainingStartTime` to `TrainingEndTime`, which is why the download and the upload are on
the bill but the wait for a GPU is not. Read that table with the previous lessons in mind and two consequences fall
out. First, a long wait in Starting (a busy GPU type) costs you time but no money. Second, **every gigabyte you copy
before training is paid at the GPU's hourly price**: a 2 TB dataset at 500 MB/s is 67 minutes of an expensive
machine doing nothing but copying. That is the reason for the next section.

The bill is instance-hours: billed time × instance count. With four instances, every billed minute counts four times,
including the four parallel downloads.

### The contract: fixed paths instead of configuration

The service and your script never talk directly. They agree on folders, a
<Term id="training-container-contract">training container contract</Term>. On SageMaker:

- **`/opt/ml/input/data/<channel>/`**: each input you pass to `fit` (a *channel*, such as `train` or `validation`)
  appears here. The environment variable `SM_CHANNEL_TRAIN` holds the path, so the script does not hard-code it.
- **`/opt/ml/model/`**: write the final model here (`SM_MODEL_DIR`). At the end, the folder is packed into
  `model.tar.gz` under your `output_path`. Anything written elsewhere is lost with the instance.
- **`/opt/ml/checkpoints/`**: when you set `checkpoint_s3_uri`, this folder is synced to S3 during the job and
  restored at the start of a restarted job. It is the "copy checkpoints to object storage" rule from the spot lesson,
  done for you.
- **`/opt/ml/output/`**: a `failure` file here becomes the job's failure reason, which saves digging through logs.

Hyperparameters arrive as command-line arguments. So a script that reads its paths from environment variables, with
local defaults, runs unchanged on a laptop and in a job. That is the whole point: the contract replaces all the
glue code you wrote by hand on the VM.

Vertex AI has the same idea with different names. Cloud Storage buckets are mounted with Cloud Storage FUSE under
`/gcs/<bucket>/`, so `gs://acme-ml/datasets/v7` reads as the folder `/gcs/acme-ml/datasets/v7`, and the variable
`AIP_MODEL_DIR` names the Cloud Storage location where the model must be saved (`AIP_CHECKPOINT_DIR` exists for
checkpoints).

### Input modes: copy first, or stream as you go

SageMaker's <Term id="training-input-mode">input mode</Term> decides how data reaches the script:

- **File** (the default) copies the whole channel to the instance's disk before the script starts. Simple, and every
  later read is local and fast, but the copy is billed and the disk must hold the dataset.
- **FastFile** presents the S3 objects as ordinary files and fetches each one on first read. Training starts almost
  at once and there is no download phase, but each first read now goes over the network during training.
- **Pipe** streams the data through a Unix pipe. It is older and needs code written for it; FastFile has mostly
  replaced it.

Which one wins depends on what the object-storage lesson called the request pattern. FastFile is excellent for **large
files read in order**, such as the WebDataset shards from that lesson: the network delivers throughput and the
latency is paid once per shard. For **millions of small files read in random order**, every first read pays S3's
time to first byte, now while the GPU waits, so removing the download can make the job slower overall. The fix is the
same as before: shard the data.

With several instances, the default `FullyReplicated` distribution gives every instance the full dataset (four
instances, four downloads); `ShardedByS3Key` gives each instance a slice, which suits data-parallel jobs that split
the data anyway.

### Managed spot training: the previous lesson as two settings

Set `use_spot_instances=True` and the job runs on spot capacity. When an instance is reclaimed, SageMaker waits for
new capacity, starts the job again, and restores `/opt/ml/checkpoints` from `checkpoint_s3_uri`, so a script that
resumes from its latest checkpoint (the loop from the spot lesson) carries on. Two limits keep it bounded:
`max_run` caps the training time and `max_wait` caps training plus waiting for capacity, so `max_wait` must be at
least `max_run`. The job record reports `TrainingTimeInSeconds` and `BillableTimeInSeconds`, and the difference is
the spot saving. Without a checkpoint configuration, every interruption restarts from step zero, which, as the spot
lesson showed, makes long jobs a trap.

<Callout kind="simplification">
The lab and the billing example use illustrative numbers: 4 minutes of unbilled start-up, one fixed transfer rate
for both download and upload, and the g6e.xlarge price used in the GPU lesson. Real start-up ranges from about a
minute (warm pools, which keep instances alive between jobs) to much longer for scarce GPUs, transfer rates depend
on instance size and file layout, and `ml.` instance prices differ from EC2 prices by region. The structure, which
phases are billed and which are not, comes from the service documentation.
</Callout>

### SageMaker and Vertex AI side by side

| Idea | SageMaker AI | Vertex AI |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The job | Training job (`Estimator.fit`) | Custom job (`CustomJob.run`) |
| Machine | Instance type, e.g. `ml.g6e.xlarge` | Machine type + accelerator, e.g. `g2-standard-8` + `NVIDIA_L4` |
| Identity | Execution role | Service account |
| Data in | `/opt/ml/input/data/<channel>` (File / FastFile / Pipe) | `/gcs/<bucket>/…` via Cloud Storage FUSE |
| Model out | `/opt/ml/model` → `model.tar.gz` | `AIP_MODEL_DIR` (a Cloud Storage path) |
| Spot | Managed spot training | Spot VMs for custom jobs (`scheduling_strategy`) |

Once the job has produced a model, both services can also host it on a managed endpoint. Serving has its own
problems (latency, autoscaling, rollouts), and the Deploy and Inference tracks cover them: see
[Kubernetes for models](#/learn/deploy/kubernetes/k8s-for-models) and
[Serving APIs and containers](#/learn/inference/serving/apis-containers). The next lesson asks a related cost
question: when is a hosted model API cheaper than any server you run?

<CommonMistake
  title="Saving the model outside the model folder"
  wrong={'torch.save(model.state_dict(), "outputs/model.pt")   # relative to the working directory'}
  error="(no error) the job completes and model.tar.gz contains no weights"
  cause="Only /opt/ml/model is packed and uploaded. Everything else on the instance is deleted when the job ends."
  fix='Save to os.environ.get("SM_MODEL_DIR", "outputs") (or AIP_MODEL_DIR on Vertex AI), with a local default so the script still runs on a laptop.'
/>

<CommonMistake
  title="Turning on spot without checkpoints"
  wrong={'PyTorch(..., use_spot_instances=True, max_run=8 * 3600, max_wait=12 * 3600)   # no checkpoint_s3_uri'}
  error="(no error) after each interruption the job restarts from step 0; long jobs hit max_wait and stop"
  cause="Without checkpoint_s3_uri nothing survives the reclaimed instance, so every restart begins again."
  fix="Set checkpoint_s3_uri, write checkpoints to /opt/ml/checkpoints, and resume from the latest one at start-up."
/>

<CommonMistake
  title="A role the job cannot pass or use"
  wrong={'role="arn:aws:iam::111122223333:role/train-job"   # caller lacks iam:PassRole'}

<Section kind="practice" title="Managed training that teams rely on">

- **Default to managed jobs for repeatable training** (fine-tunes, retraining, sweeps) and keep a VM or notebook for
  exploration. The idle tail is the biggest saving.
- **Write scripts against the contract**: read paths from `SM_CHANNEL_*`, `SM_MODEL_DIR` or `AIP_MODEL_DIR`, with
  local defaults, so the same script is tested on a laptop first.
- **Shard the data, then pick the mode**: FastFile for large sequential shards, File when the data is small or read
  randomly many times.
- **Turn on managed spot with checkpoints** for anything longer than an hour, and set `max_wait` with room for
  capacity waits.
- **Tag every job and pin its image version**, so the cost-control lesson's reports can attribute the bill and a run
  can be repeated exactly.

</Section>

<Section kind="summary">

A managed training job turns a machine you look after into a request that runs and ends. You describe the script,
the container, the instances and the data; the service provisions the machines (not billed), copies or mounts the
data and runs your script (billed), uploads the model folder (billed) and tears everything down. Because nothing is
left running, the biggest saving is usually the idle hours a VM collects, not the price per hour.

The script and the service meet at a contract of fixed paths: data under `/opt/ml/input/data`, the model in
`/opt/ml/model`, checkpoints in `/opt/ml/checkpoints` on SageMaker, and `/gcs/` plus `AIP_MODEL_DIR` on Vertex AI.
That contract is what lets one script run locally and managed. The input mode moves the data cost around (File pays
a billed download, FastFile pays per read during training), and managed spot training is the previous lesson's
checkpoint logic switched on with two settings.
